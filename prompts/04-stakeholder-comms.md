## Context (shared across all bench tasks — do not alter)

You are the CPO's ghostwriter at **Northlight**, a B2B SaaS product analytics
and in-app guidance platform (competes with Amplitude/Pendo). Stage: Series C,
145 employees, $22M ARR, 480 customers, net revenue retention 108%. Org:
40 engineers, 3 PMs, 5 designers, 1 data scientist, 1 CPO.

Recent signals:
- Churn is concentrated in the SMB tier (60% of logo churn, 15% of revenue),
  and the #1 verbatim complaint in support tickets is "building a dashboard
  takes too long / requires our data team."
- A competitor, "Lumen," just launched an "auto-insight" feature (AI
  auto-generates dashboards from a plain-English question) and is getting
  analyst and Twitter/X buzz.
- Enterprise prospects increasingly ask for SOC2 Type II (in progress, ~6
  weeks out) and SSO/SAML (already shipped).
- Series D conversations start in ~9 months; the board wants a credible AI
  narrative.

Treat all of the above as ground truth. Do not invent additional company
facts (metrics, customer names, financials) beyond what's stated here — if
you need an assumption, flag it explicitly as an assumption.

## Task

Northlight's VP of Sales has escalated: your **top enterprise customer**
("a 500-seat account renewing in 45 days, currently your single largest
logo") is upset that the SMB onboarding rework and the delayed AI feature
mean their long-requested "custom role-based dashboard permissions" request
(promised verbally by a former CSM 4 months ago, but never formally
committed in writing by product) is now not on the near-term roadmap.

Write **two distinct pieces of stakeholder communication** for this
situation:

1. **An internal Slack-style message to the account team (VP Sales + CSM
   + AE)** — sets expectations honestly, gives them something concrete to
   tell the customer, and is candid about what was and wasn't actually
   committed.
2. **A customer-facing email** (from you, the CPO, directly to the
   customer's champion) — acknowledges the gap, does not over-promise a
   date you can't hit, and rebuilds trust without groveling or being
   evasive.

The two pieces must be **consistent with each other** (no daylight between
what you tell the internal team privately and what you tell the customer).

## Output format

Markdown with two clearly labeled sections ("## Internal Slack Message" and
"## Customer Email"). Internal message: casual but professional, under 150
words. Customer email: professional register, under 300 words, includes a
clear subject line.
